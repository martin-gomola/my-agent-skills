# Phrases and Words to Review

Remove these when they delay the point or replace evidence with performance. Keep a phrase when it carries real meaning, intentional uncertainty, or recognizable voice.

## Throat-Clearing Openers

- "Here's the thing"
- "Here's what I mean"
- "Let me be clear"
- "The uncomfortable truth is"
- "It turns out"
- "The real point is"
- "I'm going to be honest"
- "Can we talk about"

State the content directly unless the opener creates genuine character or context.

## Faux-Insight and Interpretive Setups

- "What nobody tells you"
- "The part everyone misses"
- "What most people get wrong"
- "The key point is"
- "That last part matters more than it sounds"
- "As you can see"
- "In other words"

These often announce insight or instruct the reader how to interpret it. Delete the setup and let the supported claim stand.

## Empty Emphasis and Importance Puffery

- "Full stop" / "Period"
- "Let that sink in"
- "Make no mistake"
- "This changes everything"
- "marks a pivotal moment"
- "stands as a testament"
- "plays a vital role"
- "underscores its significance"
- "solidifies its position"

Replace them with the fact, mechanism, consequence, or next action that makes the point important.

## Business and Model Jargon

| Review | Prefer when accurate |
|--------|----------------------|
| Navigate challenges | Handle, address |
| Unpack an analysis | Explain, examine |
| Lean into | Accept, use, commit to |
| Landscape | Situation, field, market |
| Game-changer | Name the concrete change |
| Deep dive | Analysis, review |
| Moving forward | Next, from now |
| Circle back | Return to, revisit |
| Delve | Examine, explain |
| Leverage / utilize | Use |
| Facilitate | Help, enable |
| Robust | Name the capability or failure it withstands |

Do not rotate synonyms merely for variety. Repeat the clear term when it refers to the same thing.

## Weak and Fake-Strong Verbs

Prefer a direct verb over a padded phrase:

| Review | Prefer |
|--------|--------|
| Make a decision | Decide |
| Has the ability to | Can |
| Conduct an analysis | Analyze |
| Serves as a hub | Name what it stores, tracks, or connects |
| Acts as a reminder | Reminds |

Plain `is` and `has` are often clearer than inflated replacements.

## Adverbs, Hedges, and Qualifiers

Review words such as `really`, `just`, `literally`, `genuinely`, `honestly`, `simply`, `actually`, `deeply`, `truly`, `fundamentally`, `inherently`, `inevitably`, `importantly`, and `crucially`.

Cut them when they add empty emphasis. Keep them when they express real uncertainty, contrast, degree, timing, or the writer's natural spoken rhythm.

## Weasel Attribution

- "Experts agree"
- "Studies show"
- "Industry reports suggest"
- "Many argue"
- "Widely regarded as"

Name the source when the draft provides one. Otherwise cut or flag the claim. Never invent a source.

## Meta-Commentary and Recaps

- "The rest of this essay explains"
- "Let me walk you through"
- "In this section, we'll"
- "As we'll see"
- "In conclusion"
- "Ultimately"
- "Overall"

Cut structural announcements and summaries when the prose already makes the structure or conclusion clear.

## Model Catchphrases

Replace portable labels with the specific mechanism or risk:

| Review | State instead |
|--------|---------------|
| Load-bearing claim | The only claim the argument depends on |
| Load-bearing citation | The citation supporting this fact |
| Load-bearing test | The regression test covering this path |
| Load-bearing assumption | The assumption this change depends on |
| Not load-bearing | Stale, redundant, cosmetic, or safe to remove |

If the meaning is `single point of failure`, `critical dependency`, `required invariant`, or `only supporting evidence`, say that directly.
